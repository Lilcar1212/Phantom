# Build a slow trend bot, skip meme sniping

For a US resident with about $20, the automated strategy most likely to do best is a **long-only, low-turnover trend filter on spot Bitcoin (optionally Ether), layered on a recurring-buy (DCA) core, run with free open-source tooling on a regulated US exchange**. Time-series momentum is the one bot strategy with repeated academic support in crypto. Fees and minimum order sizes rule out nearly everything else at this account size. Grid trading has roughly zero expected value before fees. Arbitrage and market making are fee and latency races that retail traders lose. Funding-rate carry needs about $1,000 or more and its returns turned negative in 2025 in one study. ML and LLM bots show no net edge. Derivatives are now legal onshore, but their margin floors and per-contract fees exceed a $20 account. On meme coins the answer is **no to sniping and copy-trading**: they are negative expected value against professional bots, rug pulls and round-trip costs of 5–9%. A slow trend rule on a large-cap meme coin is the only defensible meme variant, and nothing has tested it net of fees. Be candid about the money. At $20, even a good strategy earns cents to a few dollars a year. The real return on this project is a tested, secure pipeline you can trust with more money later. Scale up only when that pipeline passes process gates, not because of a lucky month.

## Trend following is the only strategy with both evidence and a $20 fit

Most bot strategies look profitable before costs and fail after them. Time-series momentum (TSMOM), meaning being long an asset when its own recent trend is up and in cash when it is down, is the exception, because it collects a behavioral or risk premium rather than exploiting a mispricing that faster traders can close. Liu and Tsyvinski found crypto returns are explained mainly by crypto-specific momentum and investor attention, and they report "a strong time-series momentum effect" ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)). A 2025 practitioner paper from Concretum Group ran an ensemble of Donchian-channel trend rules across the top 20 liquid coins on survivorship-bias-free data back to 2015. It reports a **net-of-fee Sharpe above 1.5 and about 10.8% annual alpha versus BTC**, but this is a backtest, not live trading ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5209907)). The effect has not visibly decayed since spot ETFs launched: one non-peer-reviewed study puts TSMOM's Sharpe at 0.82 before January 2024 and 1.22 after, over a short window ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/19671502)). The caveat matters. Han, Kang and Ryu show that once realistic costs and intraday liquidations are modeled, many momentum portfolios that looked significant become insignificant. TSMOM survives better than cross-sectional momentum (buying the coins that rose most and shorting the laggards), whose evidence is "weak" ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4675565)). That cross-sectional version is also prone to crashes, including a −255% month in December 2020 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). An honest expectation for a cost-aware, long-only trend filter is a **Sharpe of roughly 0.8–1.5, earned mainly by sidestepping deep bear-market drawdowns rather than by beating BTC in bull markets**. Sitting in cash has a real cost: missing just the 15 best three-day BTC moves in one sample turned a +127% return into −84.6% ([Amdax](https://medium.com/amdax-asset-management/lump-sum-or-dollar-cost-averaging-42c8f5bb9938)).

Fee arithmetic decides the rest. At the lowest volume tiers, which a $20 account will never leave, round-trip costs are about **0.5–0.8% on Kraken Pro and 0.8–1.2% on Coinbase Advanced**. Binance.US is the outlier at 0% maker and 0.02% taker since April 2026 ([BusinessWire via Rutland Herald](https://www.rutlandherald.com/news/business/binance-us-slashes-spot-trading-fees-to-near-zero-for-all-users/article_03c05c3d-289a-557a-aef5-443d71beea8b.html); [Financer](https://financer.com/review/coinbase); [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/kraken-fees-explained)). A trend filter that changes position a handful of times a year pays a few percent annually. A strategy that trades weekly at Coinbase rates pays 40–60% a year in fees. That single comparison eliminates most of the field:

| Strategy | What the evidence says | Viable at $20? |
|---|---|---|
| Slow trend filter (BTC/ETH spot) | Strongest academic support; net Sharpe ~0.8–1.5 plausible ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5209907)) | **Yes**: few trades, affordable fees |
| DCA / recurring buys | Lump sum beats DCA in ~65–80% of BTC windows, but DCA cuts drawdowns ([Nakamoto Portfolio](https://nakamotoportfolio.com/static/docs/DCA_Lumpsum.pdf)) | **Yes**: the natural core if you add money from income |
| Mean reversion / pairs | No rigorous standalone study found; pairs earn 11.61%/month at 5-minute frequency but −0.07%/month at daily frequency with the distance method, and are highly cost-sensitive ([Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10293523.2023.2268386)) | No: profits need high frequency and fees retail can't get |
| Grid | Classic grid has **~zero expected return**: in-range gains offset the loss when price exits ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11921)) | Poor: fees push negative except on Binance.US |
| Market making | Small accounts "struggle" with fees, minimums and spreads ([Finestel](https://finestel.com/blog/hummingbot-review/)) | No |
| Triangular / cross-exchange arbitrage | 4,879 Binance opportunities found, none profitable after costs and depth ([Finance Research Letters](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S154461232401537X)) | No |
| Funding-rate carry | Sharpe fell from 6.45 (2020–25) to 4.06 (2024) and **turned negative in 2025** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v2)) | No: needs ~$1k+ and derivatives |
| ML / LLM bots | ML signals profitable gross, negative net ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060v1)); production LLM agents show "no directional edge" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663)) | No: LLM API costs alone can exceed returns |
| Meme sniping / copy-trading | ~98.6% of pump.fun tokens tied to rugs or pump-and-dumps ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)) | **No: negative expected value** |

The ML and LLM verdict deserves emphasis because it is the most heavily marketed option. In Nof1's Alpha Arena, six frontier LLMs traded $10,000 each on Hyperliquid perps, and four lost money, GPT by about 63% ([Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)). A larger 2026 study of about 3,500 meme coin vaults and 500-plus perp agents found the perp fleet unprofitable, with a 41% round-trip win rate against 50% for matched human retail, and volatility-blind 5x median leverage ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663)). The CFTC warns that promises of AI or arbitrage bots returning 20–50% are a fraud hallmark ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)). Use an LLM to help write and review code, not to generate signals. No audited data exists on the share of retail bots that make money. Adjacent proxies, such as copy-trading followers (48% profitable) and the production LLM agents, suggest most retail bots underperform holding BTC ([YieldFund](https://yieldfund.com/is-copy-trading-profitable-a-90-day-multi-exchange-study)).

## Meme coin sniping and copy-trading are negative-expected-value lotteries

You asked whether to focus on meme coins. The direct answer is no. The economics are worse than random because the game is rigged by faster players. Pump.fun has about 11.9 million launches, and **fewer than 2% ever graduate to a DEX** ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/pumpfun-launched-42000-tokens-in-one-day-fewer-than-2-will-ever-reach-a-dex)). Solidus Labs found 98.6% of 7 million-plus tokens showed rug-pull or pump-and-dump patterns, and fewer than about 100,000 kept even $1,000 of liquidity ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/05/07/98-of-tokens-on-pump-fun-have-been-rug-pulls-or-an-act-of-fraud-new-report-says)). A two-year analysis of all ~15 million pump.fun coins attributes 60–80% of trading volume to bots and finds manipulation tools sold "as a service" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)). Even apparent winners are mostly manufactured: **82.89% of meme tokens returning over 100% showed artificial growth** such as wash trading ([USENIX Security 2026 / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01963v2)).

Sniping is a latency race you cannot win from a home connection. In June 2026, researchers identified 1,012 persistent wallet cohorts that repeatedly land among the first buyers. The top cohort was in the first ten buyers of 42 launches in 11 days, at an average entry rank of 2.29 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)). A $20 bot on a public RPC buys after them, which means buying from them. Copy-trading has a structural "imitation penalty": even an instant copier fills at a worse point on the bonding curve. KOLs (crypto influencers) use coordinated bots to make copiers their exit liquidity ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)). Costs make it worse. Pump.fun charges 1.25% per trade on the bonding curve ([soltokencreator.io](https://www.soltokencreator.io/blog/pump-fun-fees-explained)), and Telegram bots such as Trojan, BONKbot and Photon add 0.75–1% ([solanatools.io](https://solanatools.io/blog/solana-trading-bot-fees-compared)), before slippage and priority tips. That is roughly **5.5–8.5% per round trip**. With zero edge, ten round trips turn $20 into about $9.68. Wide slippage settings also feed Solana sandwich bots, which extracted an estimated $370–500 million over about 16 months ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)).

The headline that "73% of pump.fun traders are profitable" (April 2026) does not contradict this. CoinGecko attributes the rise to losers leaving: monthly active wallets fell from 5.2 million to 1.8 million. About 65% of the "winners" made only $1–$500 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/73-pump-fun-traders-profit-094850577.html)). That is survivorship bias, not a 73% chance of profit for a newcomer. Security is its own hazard. A fake "solana-pumpfun-bot" GitHub repo with inflated stars stole users' private keys ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solana-trading-bot-github-malware-scam)), and the Banana Gun Telegram bot lost about $3 million to an exploit ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/317282/banana-gun-telegram-bot)).

If you want meme exposure anyway, the least-bad design is the same slow trend rule applied to a large-cap meme coin such as DOGE, SHIB, PEPE, BONK or WIF on a regulated US venue. These coins carry no rug risk and produce a broker 1099. Robinhood lists all five ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/robinhood-lists-3-new-meme-coins-as-crypto-trading-expands-after-trumps-election)), but its 0.85% lowest-tier fee is costly for a bot ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/crypto-fee-tiers)), so check Kraken's or Coinbase's API for which meme pairs they list. Nothing in this research backtested trend rules on meme coins net of US retail fees. Treat it as an experiment run on paper first, alongside a BTC bot rather than in place of one.

## Derivatives are legal onshore but priced out of a $20 account

US residents no longer need offshore venues for leverage. Coinbase has offered CFTC-regulated perpetual-style futures since July 21, 2025, with up to 10x leverage and a nano BTC contract of 0.01 BTC ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2025-07-22-coinbase-launches-perpetual-futures-363796)). Kraken added US perps on June 15, 2026 through its Bitnomial acquisition, with "as little as $25 intraday margin" and up to 50x leverage ([Coinpaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/news/kraken-brings-crypto-60-trillion-perpetuals/)). Each of those floors breaks a $20 account. Kraken's $25 margin minimum already exceeds it. Its fee of $0.15 per contract per side, plus a **$25 liquidation fee**, means one liquidation erases everything ([Kraken](https://support.kraken.com/articles/us-futures-fees)). One Coinbase nano contract represents hundreds of dollars or more of BTC. A hedged funding-carry trade, which pairs a spot purchase with a short perp to collect funding payments, needs that full spot leg plus a margin buffer, which works out to roughly $1,000–1,500. And the premium it harvests has collapsed ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v2)). Directional leverage on a tiny account is the fastest documented route to zero. The volatility-blind 5x leverage of the LLM-agent fleet is the cautionary example ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663)).

Do not use offshore perp venues through a VPN. Hyperliquid geoblocks US IPs, and dYdX's protocol is "not available … to U.S. persons" ([OneKey](https://onekey.so/blog/ecosystem/geo-block-hyperliquid-dydx-us/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/377073/dydx-enter-us)). Binance.com gave identified US users 14 days before locking their accounts ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/85589/binance-blocking-us-users-14-day-deadline-new-email)). OKX pleaded guilty and paid more than $504 million, partly for coaching Americans to "just put a random country" at sign-up ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/okx-pleads-guilty-violating-us-anti-money-laundering-laws-and-agrees-pay-penalties)). The user, not the exchange, bears the frozen-funds risk. The verdict is **spot only, no leverage**, until capital is in the thousands and a hedged strategy has a demonstrated premium.

## A five-phase build: Kraken, Freqtrade, fee-honest tests, then $20 live

### Kraken plus Freqtrade is the default stack, with fallbacks for New York and Coinbase users

Choose **Kraken Pro** as the exchange. It serves US residents, explicitly allows API trading bots ([Kraken](https://support.kraken.com/es/articles/360001373983)), and charges less than Coinbase at the entry tier. It is also an officially supported exchange in **Freqtrade**, the most active open-source bot framework, with about 55,000 GitHub stars and commits on the day this research checked. Freqtrade is free and includes backtesting, dry-run paper trading, hyperparameter optimization, lookahead-bias analysis, drawdown "protections", a web UI and Telegram control ([Freqtrade](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade)). On Kraken it also supports exchange-side stop-losses, which keep working if the bot dies ([Freqtrade exchange notes](https://gitcode.com/GitHub_Trending/fr/freqtrade/blob/develop/docs/exchanges.md)). Fee sources conflict: Kraken Pro's entry tier is reported as 0.25% maker / 0.40% taker in one source and 0.40% / 0.80% in another ([Kraken support](https://support.kraken.com/hc/en-us/articles/201893638)), so check your account's actual tier before setting backtest fees. Kraken does not serve New York ([Koinly](https://koinly.io/blog/best-crypto-exchanges-new-york/)), and neither does Binance.US. New York residents, or anyone who prefers Coinbase, should use Coinbase Advanced through a small custom script built on CCXT, the MIT-licensed library that underlies most of these tools and supports Coinbase, Kraken, Gemini and Binance.US ([CCXT](https://pypi.org/project/ccxt)). Hummingbot also has a Coinbase connector, but it is built for market making ([Hummingbot](https://hummingbot.org/exchanges/coinbase/)). Binance.US is cheapest and allows $1 orders ([Binance.US](https://blog.binance.us/tag/for-advanced-traders/)), but its roughly 0.20% market share and regulatory history add counterparty risk ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/binance-us-cuts-fees-near-205834438.html)). Skip Jesse, whose live trading is a paid license of about $899 ([Gurubase](https://gurubase.io/g/jesse/can-jesse-trade-live)), and backtrader, which has not been updated since August 2024.

Minimum order sizes are the binding constraint at $20. Kraken's `costmin` varies by pair; LTC/USDT, for example, requires 10 USDT ([Kraken](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/guides/spot-errors/)). A $20 account therefore supports one, maybe two, positions. Set `max_open_trades` to 1, start with BTC/USD alone, and have the code read minimums from the exchange (CCXT `market['limits']`) rather than hardcoding them.

### The phased plan

| Phase | Duration | What you do | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Accounts and compliance | Week 1 | Confirm Kraken (or Coinbase) serves your state; open account with hardware-key or TOTP 2FA; fund $20; set a tax-records folder | Account verified; you have read the API terms |
| 1. Tooling | Weeks 1–2 | Install Freqtrade via Docker locally; `download-data` for Kraken BTC/USD and ETH/USD daily and 4h candles; write a simple strategy, e.g. long when close is above a 100–200-day moving average or a Donchian breakout, flat otherwise | Strategy runs in backtest without errors |
| 2. Backtest honestly | Weeks 2–5 | Set fee to your real taker rate; test 2021 bull, 2022 bear, 2023–26 recovery; hold out the latest year untouched; run `lookahead-analysis`; log every variant tried; bootstrap the trade list for a drawdown distribution; compare against BTC buy-and-hold **and** plain DCA | Out-of-sample result beats DCA on drawdown without collapsing on return; under ~10 parameter variants tried |
| 3. Paper trade | Weeks 5–12 | Run Freqtrade dry-run on live Kraken data with Telegram alerts; verify signals, order sizing, restarts and fee accounting match the backtest | Several weeks with no unexplained divergence or crash |
| 4. Live at $20 | Month 3 onward | Trade-only, IP-locked key; MaxDrawdown and StoplossGuard protections; exchange-side stop; maker (post-only) limit orders where possible | Months of clean operation, see scaling section |

Phase 2 is where most bot projects quietly fail. Running hundreds of hyperparameter trials guarantees some impressive backtests by chance. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio exists to discount for exactly that: it adjusts a backtest's Sharpe for how many variants were tried ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). Keep the parameter count tiny and the trial count honest. Survivorship bias is mild for value-weighted portfolios but enormous for equal-weighted altcoin baskets: 0.93% versus 62.19% a year across 3,904 coins ([University of St. Gallen](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/handle/20.500.14171/108037)). Restricting the bot to BTC and ETH sidesteps most of it. Backtest on the exchange you will trade, since Kraken publishes free OHLCV history ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/how-to-get-free-full-crypto-intraday-data-2013-2025-from-kraken/)). Phase 3 has one limit to accept: a daily trend strategy may trade only once or twice in two months. Paper trading validates the plumbing, not the edge. The edge case rests on the out-of-sample backtest and the literature.

### Risk controls and hosting should cost almost nothing

Risk management at $20 is mostly structural. Use spot only, one position, a fixed stake, an exchange-side stop, and Freqtrade's MaxDrawdown protection, which halts trading past a set equity drawdown such as 20% and can be backtested with `--enable-protections` ([Freqtrade protections](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/docs/includes/protections.md)). Skip Kelly sizing: estimation error on a small trade sample makes full Kelly overbet badly ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion/)). For robustness, reconcile state with the exchange on every restart and treat a timed-out order as unknown, not failed. Freqtrade's trade database handles much of this. Hosting needs only 2 GB RAM and 2 vCPUs ([Freqtrade](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freqtrade/freqtrade/develop/README.md)). A paid VPS from €3.49 a month ([Bitdoze](https://www.bitdoze.com/md/hetzner-cloud-cost-optimized-plans.md)) costs **more per year than the entire $20 account**. Run on an always-on home PC or Raspberry Pi with Docker's `restart: unless-stopped`, or on Oracle Cloud's free ARM tier. Verify Oracle's current limits first, because one 2026 report says the free tier was cut ([Medium](https://medium.com/@imvinojanv/setup-always-free-vps-with-4-ocpu-24gb-ram-and-200gb-storage-the-ultimate-oracle-cloud-guide-bed5cbf73d34); [braindetox](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/oracle_always_free_tier_reduced_2026.html)). A cloud host also gives you a static IP, which makes API-key IP allowlisting practical.

### API keys: trade-only, IP-locked, never in git

The 2022 3Commas breach leaked about 100,000 exchange API keys and caused more than $27 million in losses. Attackers did not need withdrawal rights: they used trade-only keys to pump illiquid tokens they controlled ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/news/api-key-leaks-and-exchange-inaction-a-hapi-analysis-of-the-3commas-incident)). The lesson is that **IP allowlisting is the control that matters most**. Kraken says locking keys to your IPs "prevents almost all key misuse even if the key is leaked." Never grant Withdraw, and never combine Withdraw with "Add Withdrawal Addresses," since that pair lets a thief drain the account with the key alone ([Kraken](https://docs.kraken.com/exchange/guides/rest/api-keys)). On Coinbase, grant View and Trade, never Transfer, and confirm the scope through the key-permissions endpoint ([Coinbase](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/advanced-trade-api/rest-api/data-api/get-api-key-permissions.md)). Keep secrets in a file listed in `.gitignore` with permissions set to 600. Bind Freqtrade's web UI to localhost and reach it over an SSH tunnel. Lock Telegram control to your own chat ID. Remember that a Freqtrade strategy file is arbitrary Python with access to your keys, so run only code you have read. Self-hosting removes the third-party-platform risk that sank 3Commas users.

### US legal and tax steps

Running a bot on your own account at a US venue is legal. The legal lines are manipulation (wash trading, spoofing, coordinated pumps), which the DOJ has prosecuted to prison sentences ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/325111/gotbit-founder-sentenced-prison-crypto-wash-trading)), and evading offshore geoblocks. For tax, crypto is property, so **every bot sale is a taxable event**, and nearly all bot gains will be short-term, taxed at ordinary rates of 10–37% ([SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/how-is-crypto-taxes/)). Since 2025, basis is tracked wallet by wallet under Rev. Proc. 2024-28, so treat the bot's exchange account as its own ledger ([Koinly](https://koinly.io/blog/irs-revenue-procedure-crypto-depot/)). US exchanges issue Form 1099-DA, and for assets acquired in 2026 or later they must report cost basis too ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1099da.pdf)). Notice 2026-20 lets your own lot-selection records control through December 31, 2026, even where they differ from the broker's 1099-DA ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/03/tnf-notice-2026-20-one-year-extension-of-temporary-relief-for-making-adequate-identification-of-units-of-digital-assets-held-in-brokers-custody.html)). The wash-sale rule does not apply to crypto today. However, both the Lummis bill (S.2207) and a December 2025 House draft would extend it, and the Senate version could reach 2026 trades ([Lummis](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/press-releases/lummis-unveils-digital-asset-tax-legislation/); [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/January/December-29-January-5-Tax-Policy-Update)). Log every fill with its timestamp, fee and lot so you can recompute if the rules change. A low-turnover trend bot is also the tax-friendly choice. It produces a few dozen disposals a year, well under the 100-transaction tier of tools like Koinly ($49) ([Koinly](https://support.koinly.io/en/articles/9856841-tax-plans)) and few enough to enter on Form 8949 by hand. A grid or meme bot would generate thousands. Trader tax status and the §475(f) mark-to-market election are a contested grey area for spot crypto and are not worth pursuing at this size ([CoinTracker](https://www.cointracker.io/blog/write-off-unlimited-losses)). The CLARITY Act stalled after a 49–50 Senate cloture vote on September 15, 2026, so token classification remains unsettled ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation)). That does not affect a spot BTC bot. Note: this is informational, not tax advice.

## Scale capital on process gates, because live P&L proves nothing for months

The counterintuitive point about scaling is that **live returns on a slow trend bot are statistically meaningless for a long time**. A strategy that trades a few times a year will not produce enough trades to separate skill from luck for years. Scaling should therefore depend on evidence you can actually check. That means an out-of-sample backtest that survived honest costs and a small trial count. It means live fills, fees and signals that match paper trading. And it means a quarter or more of live operation with no security, crash or reconciliation incidents. Clear those gates and capital can grow in steps (the dollar thresholds below are this report's estimates, not sourced figures), using only money you can afford to lose. At roughly **$200–500**, BTC and ETH can run simultaneously above minimum order sizes, and volatility-based position sizing becomes feasible. At roughly **$1,000–2,000**, a top-10 to top-20 trend rotation like Concretum's becomes practical, and a $4–6 monthly VPS falls to a few percent of capital. Around $1,000–1,500 is also the floor for hedged funding carry. Revisit carry only if live funding data show the premium has recovered from its 2025 negative turn. Better fee tiers start at $10,000 of 30-day volume ([Financer](https://financer.com/review/coinbase)), which a small account would have to turn over about 500 times a month to reach. Plan on entry-tier fees indefinitely. Leverage, sniping and LLM signal bots stay off the table at every size covered here.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. With $20, picking the "best" strategy matters less than avoiding the strategies that are structurally negative: high-turnover bots that fees consume, meme sniping where professional cohorts and rug-pullers sit on the other side, and leverage that a single liquidation fee can wipe out. What survives is boring by design: a slow, long-only trend filter on BTC, held in a fee-cheap regulated account, tested against its own overfitting, and secured with an IP-locked trade-only key. The edge comes from the rule's restraint, not from cleverness or speed.

The $20 phase also buys something more valuable than its returns. It is cheap tuition in the failure modes that cost larger accounts real money: lookahead bias, fee blindness, key leaks, restart bugs and tax-lot chaos. The pipeline you build now is the actual asset. The capital is just the test load.
